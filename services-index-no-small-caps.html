Forensic & legal linguistics

If you want to learn more about what forensic and legal linguistics is, please go to: IAFLL — Forensic Linguistics ↗

My academic formation, expertise and experience can help you with the following in this field:

i) analyses of recordings or transcripts of police interrogations and interviews of suspects, witness, and victims (including individuals vulnerable due to protected characteristics such as ethnicity, race, nationality, language competency and gender)

ii) analyses of recorded or transcribed depositions of experts that are damaging for your client (eg. depositions of forensic psychologists who assessed the mental health of your client in an insurance claim and accused your client of malingering)

iii) analyses of court transcripts to detect linguistic, cultural and general communication difficulties that may have created misunderstandings impacting your case

iv) authorship identification and attribution, plagiarism detection, trademark infringement, defamation and libel

A couple of examples: In one case I analysed a transcribed police interrogation in which I discovered that the alleged admission by a suspect was not voluntary and in another I analysed vast electronic datasets in order to prevent a big tech company from trademarking a word that would prevent my client from doing business and continuing to use that specific word that almost got trademarked.

Expert Witnessing

I prepare written reports and testify in court to help you win the case. My expert witnessing is independent, evidence-based and scientific. For example, your client has been deemed dishonest and deceptive but my analysis of the available linguistic evidence may be able to assess whether that was actually the case and whether your client may have misunderstood something instead.

Evidential detail can be lost in translation, easily. The high-stake contexts such as legal are particularly conducive to this loss. And before you ask, no, AI is not there yet to ensure safe, accurate and reliable transfers of meaning between languages in sensitive contexts such as that of the law. But there is more to it. Linguistic and cultural differences can affect not only the content of the linguistic evidence elicited but also the memory and judgments related to it. If expert opinion along these lines matters for your case, you are at the right place. For example, your client may have been questioned by the police via an interpreter and you want to ensure that there was no miscommunication or contamination of linguistic evidence. That interpreter may have been another police officer, unqualified to perform the interpreting. Or you noticed that your client has been questioned in the native language by the police officer that speaks that language but not fluently because he is a heritage speaker and your client had communication difficulty as a result.

A real-life example- a suspect was denying something in the original language while in translation this was taken to be a confession! I studied and wrote about this extensively and introduced this phenomenon into the field of forensic and legal psychology as "inadvertent confessions". You can read more about it here.

Police & Investigative Training

I train investigators so that they do not end up having me on the opposing side scrutinizing the linguistic evidence they had elicited. I use a unique database consisting of authentic police video recorded and transcribed interviews and interrogations from the UK and US respectively (over 200+cases and counting). If you want your investigative teams to learn from real-life cases and learn how to elicit best evidence that is reliable and that can stand in court, you are at the right place. Crucially, I offer this training with examples in many different languages as my dataset is multilingual and multicultural so investigating across different languages and cultures becomes much easier for you and your team. Finally, together we ensure that nobody false-confesses or force-confesses - this is a goal very close to my heart as I am dedicated to helping improve justice systems around the world by keeping the real culprits in and the innocent out of incarceration via clever questioning methods that I champion and teach.
